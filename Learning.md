# Learning Cards for Your Hypotheses

## Learning Card 1: Environmental Footprint Badge Awareness  
**HYPOTHESIS**  
Displaying per-item environmental footprint badges will reduce purchase intent.

**OBSERVATION**  
There was a 15% decrease in purchase-intent rate among users exposed to the footprint badges.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**  
- Users responded more strongly to water-savings data (“25,000 L saved”) than carbon figures.  
- Some users felt overwhelmed by technical carbon metrics.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**  
- Promote the water badge prominently on both product listings and detail pages.  
- Simplify the carbon tooltip (e.g., “Equivalent to driving 15 km”) for relatability.  
- Add a “What does this mean?” info link that explains metrics in layperson terms.

## Learning Card 2: Sales Catalog Conversion  
**HYPOTHESIS**  
12% of “Add to Cart” actions will convert to a purchase in the same session.

**OBSERVATION**  
Only 10% of add-to-cart actions converted; 25% drop-off occurred at the payment step.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**  
- Even with a two-step checkout, new users found it lengthy and confusing.  
- Users requested a clearer progress indicator and confirmation that their cart contents would be saved.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**  
- Introduce a dynamic progress bar (“Shipping ▶ Payment ▶ Review”) to orient users.  
- Add a persistent “Continue as Guest” tooltip to clarify the guest-checkout flow.  
- Combine address fields and auto-detect city from ZIP code to reduce typing.

## Learning Card 3: Recycling Drop-off Map Interaction  
**HYPOTHESIS**  
30% of map searches will become detail-view clicks.

**OBSERVATION**  
Detail-view rate was 28%; many mobile users found smaller map pins hard to tap.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**  
- Map markers need to be larger and use contrasting colors on both desktop and mobile.  
- Clustering confused some users, who thought there were fewer locations available.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**  
- Increase pin size by 20% and add a subtle hover/bounce animation to emphasize interactivity.  
- Adjust clustering so clusters only form when zoomed far out; add “Zoom in to see all” hint.  
- Display a “List View” callout for users struggling to click the map.

## Learning Card 4: Pickup Scheduler Adoption  
**HYPOTHESIS**  
20% of page visitors will schedule a pickup within 10 days.

**OBSERVATION**  
Scheduling rate reached 18%; many users were unsure which slots were morning versus afternoon.

**LEARNING AND INSIGHTS**  
- The Flatpickr calendar looked busy; available slots were easily overlooked.  
- Grouping slots under “Morning” and “Afternoon” improved clarity in user interviews.

**DECISIONS AND ACTIONS**  
- Partition available slots into collapsible groups: “Morning (8 AM – 12 PM)” and “Afternoon (1 PM – 5 PM)”.  
- Highlight “Next Available” slot above the calendar in bold.  
- Add a tooltip on hover: “Slots open 48 hours before pickup date.”
